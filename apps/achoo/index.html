THE SAMPLING-THEORY VIEW

What does a test tell us?

A p-value measures how unusual a test statistic at least this extreme would be under equal disease risks. It is not the probability that A is better, or that the null hypothesis is true.

TestStatisticp-valueAt α = 0.05

The conditional Pearson test enumerates all tables with these margins: the exact reference distribution estimated by the script’s 100,000 simulations. Yates uses the continuity-corrected χ² approximation. Logistic regression uses a two-sided Wald z test for Group B.

Under the hood · assumptions & the R script

The Bayesian model assumes independent Bernoulli outcomes within each group and uses the default priors from the R script’s brms logistic regression.

The model matches get_prior() and make_stancode() for your Disease ~ Group model in installed brms 2.22.0: a Student-t prior with 3 degrees of freedom, location 0, and scale 2.5 on the centered intercept, with a flat prior on GroupB. Let ηA = logit(pA), ηB = logit(pB), and w = nB / (nA + nB). The centered intercept is (1 − w)ηA + wηB; GroupB is ηB − ηA. Changing the allocation therefore also changes how this prior acts on the two risks.

The flat slope prior is improper: it has no finite normalizing constant. There is no normalized joint prior on disease risks to plot or draw prior-predictive samples from. The posterior can still be proper; completely separated groups make it improper here, and the app flags this instead of reporting probabilities.

Browser calculations use deterministic numerical integration of this model, not MCMC. The brms curves and summaries approximate the posterior that the R script samples; they are not exported brms draws. Integration uses transformed, unbounded log-odds axes. For boundary counts, two grid resolutions are compared; unresolved results are withheld. Boundary-case brms density curves are omitted because mass near 0 or 1 is difficult to display reliably.

Density axes show the central posterior range, so extreme tails may be off-screen. Probabilities integrate the full domain. CrI means an equal-tailed credible interval. All posterior statements are conditional on this model and data.

A significant two-sided test provides evidence of a difference. Its observed direction may favor A or B; failure to reject does not establish equal effectiveness. See the brms prior documentation for the centered-intercept and flat-coefficient conventions.
