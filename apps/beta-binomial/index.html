How it works · assumptions & the R example

Read the step-by-step conjugacy derivation with LaTeX equations →

We model independent Bernoulli trials with a common success probability θ. Their total Y has a Binomial(n, θ) distribution. A Beta(α, β) prior gives a Beta(α + y, β + n − y) posterior. The labels “success” and “failure” simply mean outcomes 1 and 0.

The initial values reproduce beta_binomial_complete.R: α = β = 10 and observations (1, 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 0, 1, 1, 1). Seven successes and three failures give Beta(17, 13). The prior/posterior plot extends the R example with a separate likelihood and posterior predictive distribution.

The posterior mean combines the prior mean with the observed success fraction. Their weights are α + β and n. Prior concentration is a weight in this formula; it need not represent actual past participants. With zero observations, the posterior equals the prior.

For m future trials, P(K = k | data) = choose(m, k) × B(α′ + k, β′ + m − k) / B(α′, β′), where α′ and β′ are posterior shapes and B is the beta function. This averages over uncertainty in θ rather than fixing θ at its mean.

All calculations are deterministic and run locally. Credible intervals use numerical inversion of the beta CDF. Densities with a shape below 1 diverge at an endpoint; the plot clips those peaks and marks them, while probability calculations retain the full distribution.
